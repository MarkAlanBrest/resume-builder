📌 What You're Doing in This Assignment

Since you haven't held a job yet, you'll write a paragraph describing the skills, techniques, and knowledge you gained through your trade school program. This is completely normal — many trade school students are entering the workforce for the first time.

Skills & Training Paragraph
Write a paragraph in your own words about what you learned in your program. Be specific and honest — this will be used to build the Skills section of your resume.

✏️ What to Write About

  • Tools and equipment you learned to use (name them specifically)
  • Techniques and skills you practiced in class or the shop
  • Safety procedures and industry standards you were trained on
  • Hands-on tasks you can perform on your own
  • Soft skills developed: teamwork, attention to detail, time management

⚠ Important: Be Accurate

Write only about what you actually learned in your program. Do not exaggerate or include skills you haven't been trained on. This paragraph will be used directly in your resume — accuracy is essential for your professional reputation.

📋 How to Complete This Assignment
What You're Doing

You are entering your work history so it can be used to build your resume. For each job you've held, fill in the employer name, your job title, dates, and a set of bullet points that describe what you actually did there. Be honest and specific — this information will go directly on your resume.

The Bullet Points Are the Most Important Part

Bullet points are how employers learn what you can do. A weak bullet point says very little. A strong bullet point names exactly what you did, what tools or equipment you used, and the scope of the work. You need at least 3 per job — 4 or 5 is even better.

Rule 1 — Always Start With an Action Verb

Every bullet must begin with a strong action verb — a word that describes something you did. Never start with "I" or "Was responsible for."

MaintainedOperatedAssistedPerformedInstalledInspectedRepairedCleanedAssembledLoadedMonitoredPreparedProcessedHandledCompletedTrainedOrganizedDeliveredStockedScheduled
Rule 2 — Be Specific, Not Vague

Name the actual tools, equipment, systems, or tasks. The more specific you are, the more useful it is on a resume.

Weak Used equipment in the shop.
Strong Operated hydraulic lifts, diagnostic scan tools, and tire mounting equipment.
Weak Helped customers.
Strong Assisted customers with product selection and processed transactions using the POS system.
Rule 3 — Add Numbers or Volume When You Can

If you can add a number — how many, how often, how fast — it makes the bullet much stronger. Even a rough estimate is better than nothing.

Weaker Performed oil changes on customer vehicles.
Stronger Performed oil changes and tire rotations on 6–10 customer vehicles per shift.
Rule 4 — Never Write "I"

Resume bullet points never start with "I." Just start with the verb.

Wrong I cleaned the work area at the end of each shift.
Right Cleaned and organized the work area at the end of each shift following safety procedures.
Rule 5 — Think About What You Learned, Not Just What You Were Told to Do

Even if a task felt basic, describe it in a way that shows you understood it. "Stocked shelves" is fine. "Stocked and rotated inventory of 200+ SKUs to maintain product availability" shows more awareness of the job.

✏️ Example Bullet Points by Industry

These are examples of strong bullet points from various fields. Use them as a guide — write about your actual experience in your own words.

Automotive / Mechanical
  • Performed oil changes, tire rotations, and multi-point inspections on 8–10 vehicles per shift
  • Operated hydraulic lifts, diagnostic scan tools, and tire mounting equipment
  • Assisted lead technician with brake pad replacement and suspension component diagnostics
  • Maintained a clean, organized work bay and followed all OSHA shop safety procedures
  • Documented vehicle service records and communicated repair status to service advisors
Retail / Customer Service
  • Processed customer transactions and returns using a POS register system
  • Stocked and rotated product inventory across 12 departments to maintain shelf availability
  • Assisted customers with product selection and answered questions about store policies
  • Maintained a clean and organized sales floor throughout each shift
  • Handled cash and balanced a register drawer at the end of each shift
Construction / Trades
  • Assisted journeyman electrician with residential wiring, outlet installation, and panel work
  • Operated hand and power tools including drills, circular saws, and nail guns
  • Loaded, unloaded, and organized materials and equipment on job sites
  • Followed blueprints and supervisor instructions to complete assigned tasks on schedule
  • Maintained a safe and clean job site in compliance with OSHA standards
Food Service / Hospitality
  • Prepared and served food orders for up to 80 customers per shift following health codes
  • Operated kitchen equipment including fryers, grills, and commercial dishwashers
  • Restocked food prep stations and maintained inventory of supplies throughout the shift
  • Cleaned and sanitized kitchen surfaces, equipment, and dining areas per health regulations
  • Assisted with opening and closing duties including cash count and equipment shutdown
Healthcare / CNA / Medical Assistant
  • Assisted nursing staff with patient care including bathing, dressing, and mobility support
  • Monitored and recorded patient vital signs every 4 hours and reported changes to the RN
  • Maintained patient rooms in clean, organized condition following infection control standards
  • Transported patients to and from appointments and diagnostic testing areas
  • Communicated effectively with patients, families, and care team members
📊 How This Assignment Is Graded

Your submission is scored on four categories, each worth 25%. A fully completed entry with solid bullet points will earn a B or better.

Completeness25%

All required fields are filled in: employer name, job title, start date, and at least 3 bullet points. Optional fields (city, state, end date, extra bullets) are not required for full credit.

A — All fields complete, 3+ bullets
B — Minor optional field missing
D/F — Required fields left blank
Action Verbs25%

Each bullet point should begin with an action verb (Maintained, Operated, Performed, etc.). The more bullets that follow this rule, the higher the score.

A — Most or all bullets start with an action verb
B — About half use action verbs
D/F — Bullets start with "I" or no verb at all
Specificity & Detail25%

Bullets should describe actual tasks, tools, or equipment — not just vague phrases. A short but specific bullet scores higher than a long vague one.

A — Specific tools, tasks, or numbers mentioned
B — Reasonable detail, could be more specific
D/F — Bullets are one or two generic words
Professionalism25%

Bullets should be written professionally — no slang, no "I", and generally clear sentences. Minor grammar issues are fine and won't lower your grade significantly.

A — Professional tone, no "I", clear writing
B — Minor grammar issues, mostly professional
D/F — Heavy slang, very hard to understand
